# Facebook Ads Live Build

A 43-slide workshop by Gareth Winter (Head of Content & Creative Director, Mindvalley) on building Facebook ads with Claude: brand kit, insight, static ads, test and learn, then video.

## How to use this deck with Claude

Give Claude the link to this file (or the slides page) and say:

> Read this deck. Then take me through it slide by slide and apply each slide to my business: [what you sell, who buys it, your website]. Ask me one question at a time when you need information only I have. At the end, give me my brand kit, three insights, nine Facebook ad drafts (three angles x three variations) and a 15-second Reels script for the strongest one.

For the full guided build, install the four skills from the `skills/` folder.

Speaker notes: Yes, the best insights start from a human need or want, but the need alone is data: everyone wants to be safe. ### Slide 24

How Facebook decides who sees your ad
On Facebook, the creative is the targeting.
01
Pick the objective first
Sales or Leads. Facebook optimises for exactly what you ask for, so ask for the result you want, not clicks.
02
Go broad
Let Facebook find the people. Narrow interest targeting matters far less than it used to.
03
Feed it variety
Genuinely different angles, not tiny tweaks. Each new angle finds a new pocket of people.
Give it something to learn from
Install the Meta Pixel and the Conversions API, so Facebook can see who actually buys and find more people like them.
24  ·  Check Meta Business Help Centre for current guidance

Speaker notes: The big shift in Facebook ads: the algorithm now does most of the targeting, and it reads your creative to decide who to show it to (Meta's newer retrieval system, Andromeda, leans on this heavily). A pillow ad about snoring partners finds couples; one about night shifts finds nurses. So the brand kit and insights decide your audience as much as any targeting setting. Practical rules: choose Sales or Leads as the campaign objective, use broad audiences and Advantage+ placements, and give each ad set several genuinely different ads rather than one ad with ten button colours. Statics are the cheapest way to supply that variety. Set up the Pixel and Conversions API before spending. Platform behaviour changes often: check Meta's current help pages before the session.

Speaker notes: Angles (previous slide) are the reason to care; narratives are the shape of the ad. Any angle can wear any narrative, which is how three angles become many ads. PAS and Before-After-Bridge are the workhorses of direct response. Hook, Story, Offer is Russell Brunson's. Customer-as-hero is Donald Miller's StoryBrand: the customer is Luke, you are Yoda. Speaker notes: The horse-racing logic: each round is a race with one variable. The winner of each race carries forward and everything else is locked. Round 1 tests the message (the angle), because the message matters most and is cheapest to change. Round 2 keeps the winning headline and tests the visual. Rules for each race: same audience, same budget, same dates; pick the metric (usually cost per result) before launch; give it enough spend to judge; stop the losers. Facebook rule of thumb: an ad set needs roughly 50 results (purchases or leads) in a week to leave the learning phase, so budget each round for that or judge on a cheaper earlier metric such as cost per landing-page view.
